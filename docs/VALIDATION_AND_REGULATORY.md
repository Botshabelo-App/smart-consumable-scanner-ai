# Validation Methodology and Regulatory Documentation

**Status:** Release Candidate 1 (RC1) — Pilot Evaluation and Maintenance Mode. This document will be updated with final dataset size, model version, and validation metrics before any v1.0.0 release.

This document supports future discussions with regulators, customers, and auditors by describing the AI validation approach, datasets, limitations, and risk controls.

## 1. Validation Methodology

### 1.1 Scope

The Smart Consumable Scanner AI assesses the **external, visible condition** of consumable products using a smartphone camera and server-side deep-learning models. It does not claim to detect internal or sealed conditions that are not visually observable.

### 1.2 Ground-truth collection

- Each product image is labelled with:
  - Product category (e.g., produce, dairy, meat, beverage, packaged).
  - Condition class (fresh, near_expiry, suspicious, expired, damaged_packaging).
  - Product name and manufacturer (where available).
  - Capture metadata: lighting, camera model, angle, date/time, GPS.
- Labels are assigned by trained inspectors and reviewed by a second annotator before inclusion.
- Disagreements are escalated to a domain expert.

### 1.3 Train / validation / test splits

- A stratified 5-fold cross-validation is used during model development.
- A held-out test set is never used for hyperparameter tuning.
- The `ai-service/datasets/train.py` script records the exact random seed, folds, and class distribution.

### 1.4 Metrics

For every model we report:

- Per-class precision, recall, F1-score and support.
- Overall accuracy and macro/micro averages.
- Confusion matrix.
- Inference latency (mean, p50, p95, throughput).
- Model size and parameter count.
- Calibration: reliability diagram and Expected Calibration Error (ECE).

### 1.5 Statistical rigour

- Confidence intervals for F1 are computed via bootstrap resampling when sample size permits.
- Models are not released until they are evaluated on a held-out test set that is representative of the intended deployment environment.
- No synthetic or fabricated results are reported. All metrics are derived from real model inference on labelled images.

### 2.1 Public sources

| marcusklasson/GroceryStoreDataset | GitHub / arXiv | Product recognition for packaged goods and produce | MIT |

### 2.2 Internal data collection

- Pilot partners upload labelled images through the review workflow.
- Approved reviewer labels are exported with `ai-service/scripts/build_feedback_dataset.py`.
- All internal images require explicit consent and are stored in a dedicated object bucket with access logging.

### 2.3 Dataset quality checks

`ai-service/datasets/quality.py` runs on every release and checks for:

- Corrupted or unreadable files.
- Duplicate images (perceptual hash).
- Blurred or low-detail images (normalized Laplacian variance).
- Class imbalance and folder structure violations.
- Annotation consistency.

## 3. Model Architecture and Limitations

### 3.1 Model architecture

| Component | Model | Purpose |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Object detection | YOLOv8-nano | Locate products in the camera frame |
| Product category | EfficientNet-B0 | Classify product type |
| Spoilage / condition | MobileNetV3-Small | Detect visible spoilage cues |
| Packaging analysis | OpenCV heuristics | Detect swelling, leaks, damage, bruising |

### 3.2 Capabilities

The system can identify:

- Common produce (fruits, vegetables) and visible spoilage (discolouration, mould, bruising).
- Packaging damage and swelling on labelled products.
- Product category for many grocery items.
- Expiry/AI discrepancies when barcode/QR data is available.

### 3.3 Known limitations

- A standard smartphone camera cannot see through sealed or opaque packaging, cans, cartons, frozen blocks, or opaque bottles.
- It cannot detect odour, bacterial load, chemical contamination, or gas composition.
- For sealed products, the AI assessment is limited to external packaging condition and any provided metadata.
- Confidence scores reflect model certainty; they are not a guarantee of safety or regulatory compliance.

## 4. Performance Metrics

## 6. Risk Assessment

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| Model misclassifies spoiled product as fresh | Medium | High | Never use AI as sole evidence; flag low-confidence results; human review workflow; disclaimers |
| Camera cannot assess sealed product | High | Medium | Clear UI limitations; require inspector override; support NIR/hyperspectral in roadmap |
| Dataset bias (lighting, skin colour, packaging design) | Medium | Medium | Diverse data collection, demographic/region stratification, augmentation |
| Adversarial expiry label tampering | Low | High | AI + barcode cross-check + discrepancy flag + audit log |
| Device overheating / battery drain during long inspection | Medium | Low | FPS throttling, batch mode, performance monitoring |
| Privacy exposure of inspection images | Low | High | Encrypted storage, role-based access, audit logs, retention policy |

## 7. Compliance Notes

- The system is a decision-support tool, not a certified laboratory test.
- Inspectors retain final authority and must confirm AI findings before enforcement action.
- All AI outputs are logged and auditable.
- Future regulatory submissions will include this validation package plus independent third-party model validation.
